The Levy–Perdew–Sahni (LPS) theory is a rigorous formulation of [[Density Functional Theory|DFT]] that describes the ground-state [[Electron Density|density]] of a many-electron system through a [[Schrödinger Equation|Schrödinger]]-like differential equation for the square root of the density, $\rho^{1/2}(\textbf{r})$.
## Formulation
The theory establishes that the square root of the density obeys a direct eigenvalue equation:$$\begin{gather}h_{\text{eff}}([\rho];\textbf{r})\rho^{1/2}(\textbf{r})=\mu\rho^{1/2}(\textbf{r}),\\[10pt]h_{\text{eff}}([\rho];\textbf{r}) = -\frac{1}{2}\nabla^2+v_{\text{ext}}(\textbf{r})+v_{\text{eff}}([\rho];\textbf{r}),\end{gather}$$where $v_{\text{ext}}(\textbf{r})$ is the external ([[Electron-Nuclear Energy|electron-nuclear]]) potential and $v_{\text{eff}}([\rho];\textbf{r})$ is a local or multiplicative effective potential. The eigenvalue $\mu$, is the [[Chemical Potential|chemical potential]], which is the negative of the [[Ionization Energy|ionization energy]] in the exact density-functional theory.
