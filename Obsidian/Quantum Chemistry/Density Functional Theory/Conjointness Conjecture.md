The conjecture is often used to guide the development of the [[Kinetic Energy]] [[Generalized Gradient Approximation]] functionals [1], and has a form $$E_{\text{x}}[\rho]=\int\epsilon^{\text{HEG}}_{\text{x}}F_{\text{x}}(s)d\textbf{r}\leftrightarrow T_{\text{s}}[\rho]=\int\tau^{\text{HEG}}_{\text{s}}F_{\text{s}}(s)d\textbf{r},$$where $E_{\text{x}}[\rho]$ and $T_{\text{s}}[\rho]$ is the [[Exchange Energy|exchange energy]] and the [[Kinetic Energy|kinetic energy]] functionals, $\epsilon^{\text{HEG}}_{\text{x}}$ and $\tau^{\text{HEG}}_{\text{s}}$ is the exchange and kinetic energy densities of the [[Homogeneous Electron Gas|homogeneous electron gas]], $s$ is the [[Reduced Density Gradient|reduced density gradient]], and the exchange and kinetic [[Enhancement Factor|enhancement factors]] $F_{\text{x}}(s)$ and $F_{\text{s}}(s)$ are assumed to have the same functional form [2-4].

The conjecture is especially important in the context of variational calculations based on [[Euler–Lagrange]] minimization of the total energy. In such calculations, $T_{\text{s}}[\rho]$ always appears together with $E_{\text{x}}[\rho]$. Therefore, the accuracy of such approaches is determined directly by the accuracy of the sum $T_{\text{s}}[\rho]$ and $E_{\text{x}}[\rho]$ rather than the accuracy of the individual functionals $T_{\text{s}}[\rho]$ or $E_{\text{x}}[\rho]$ [4].

The conjecture has not been formally proven [4].
