\section{Main Text}

A local hybrid exchange functional is given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq: Local Hybrid Exchange}
    E^{\text{LH}}_{\text{x}}
    =
    \int
    \left\{
        g(\textbf{r})
        e_{\text{x}}^{\text{exact}}(\textbf{r})
        +
        [1-g(\textbf{r})]
        e_{\text{x}}^{\text{SL}}(\textbf{r})
    \right\}
    d\textbf{r},
\end{equation}
where the integrand is a mixture of two exchange density energies weighed by a local mixing function (LMF), $g(\mathbf{r})$, which is position-dependent. The $e_{\text{x}}^{\text{SL}}$ and $e_{\text{x}}^{\text{exact}}$ quantities are a semi-local and the exact exchange energy densities, respectively. The exact exchange density (closed-shell) is given by
\begin{equation}
    e_{\text{x}}^{\text{exact}}(\mathbf{r})
    =
    -
    \frac{1}{4}
    \int
    \frac{|\gamma(\mathbf{r};\mathbf{r}')|^2}
    {|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|}
    d\mathbf{r}',
\end{equation}
where 
\begin{equation}
    \gamma(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')
    =
    2\sum^{N/2}_{i=1}
    \varphi^*_i(\mathbf{r})\varphi_i(\mathbf{r}')
\end{equation}
is one-electron reduced density matrix~\cite{Staroverov.2012.Matter,Parr.1989.density}.

Here, we propose a new local mixing function for local hybrid functionals. This LMF is the ratio between the Fermi--Amaldi and the exact exchange energy densities:
\begin{equation}
    g(\mathbf{r})
    =
    \frac{e_{\text{x}}^{\text{FA}}}{e_{\text{x}}^{\text{exact}}}.
\end{equation}
The Fermi--Amaldi~\cite{Fermi.1934.MRAI.6.117,Benilan.1992.186.25} energy density is expressed via electron density, $\rho(\mathbf{r})\geq 0$, and electron number, $N$,
\begin{equation}
    e_{\text{x}}^{\text{FA}}(\mathbf{r})
    =
    -
    \frac{1}{2N}
    \int
    \frac
    {\rho(\mathbf{r})\rho(\mathbf{r}')}
    {|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|}
    d\mathbf{r}'.
\end{equation} 

Both exchange densities are manifestly non-positive and equivalent for one-electron systems and two-electron singlets. Although, one can show that
\begin{equation}
    e_{\text{x}}^{\text{FA}}
    >
    e_{\text{x}}^{\text{exact}}
    \quad
    \text{when}
    \quad
    N>2
\end{equation}
by considering corresponding exchange holes. The exchange hole is defined via
\begin{equation}
    E_{\text{x}}
    =
    \frac{1}{2}
    \int\int
    \frac
    {\rho(\mathbf{r})\rho_{\text{x}}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')}
    {|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|}
    d\mathbf{r}
    d\mathbf{r}'
\end{equation}
leading to expressions for the exact and the Fermi--Amaldi exchange holes:
\begin{equation}
    \rho^{\text{exact}}_{\text{x}}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')
    =
    -
    \frac
    {|\gamma(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')|^2}
    {2\rho(\mathbf{r})}
\end{equation}
and 
\begin{equation}
    \rho^{\text{FA}}_{\text{x}}(\mathbf{r}')
    =
    -
    \frac{\rho(\mathbf{r}')}{N}.
\end{equation}
Now, compare diagonal elements ($\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{r}'$) of the exact exchange hole to the Fermi--Amaldi exchange hole:
\begin{equation}
    \rho^{\text{FA}}_{\text{x}}
    >
    \left.
    \rho^{\text{exact}}_{\text{x}}
    \right|_{\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{r}'}
    \quad
    \text{when}
    \quad
    N
    >
    2.
\end{equation}
Since, $\rho^{\text{exact}}_{\text{x}}$ off-diagonal elements are negative, adding them to the diagonal part makes $\rho^{\text{exact}}_{\text{x}}$ even more negative. Thus, for any number of electrons:
\begin{equation}
    e_{\text{x}}^{\text{exact}}
    \leq
    e_{\text{x}}^{\text{FA}}
    \leq0.
\end{equation}
Combining these observations, we find that the proposed LMF is bound between zero and one:
\begin{equation}
    0
    \leq
    \frac{e_{\text{x}}^{\text{FA}}}
    {e_{\text{x}}^{\text{exact}}}
    \leq
    1.
\end{equation}
This inequality ensures that one hundred percent of exact exchange is employed in Eq.~\eqref{eq: Local Hybrid Exchange} for one-electron systems, satisfying the one-electron constraint~\cite{Jaramillo.2003.JCP.118.1068,Maier.2018.WIRCMS.9.e1378}.

A particular case of a one-electron limit, i.e., the asymptotic limit ($r\to\infty$) where the exact exchange must cancel out an excessive self-repulsion of the Coulomb potential, is also satisfied:
\begin{equation}
    \lim_{r\to\infty}
    \frac{e_{\text{x}}^{\text{FA}}}
    {e_{\text{x}}^{\text{exact}}}
    =
    1
\end{equation}
since both energy densities approach $-1/2r$ as $r\to\infty$.
