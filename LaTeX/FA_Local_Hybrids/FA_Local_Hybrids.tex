\section{Main Text}

Consider the exact exchange energy density (closed-shell):
\begin{equation}
    e_{\text{x}}^{\text{exact}}(\mathbf{r})
    =
    -
    \frac{1}{4}
    \int
    \frac{|\gamma(\mathbf{r};\mathbf{r}')|^2}
    {|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|}
    d\mathbf{r}',
\end{equation}
where 
\begin{equation}
    \gamma(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')
    =
    2\sum^{N/2}_{i=1}
    \varphi^*_i(\mathbf{r})\varphi_i(\mathbf{r}')
\end{equation}
is one-electron reduced density matrix~\cite{Staroverov.2012.Matter,Parr.1989.density}, and the Fermi--Amaldi~\cite{Fermi.1934.MRAI.6.117,Benilan.1992.186.25} energy density expressed via electron density, $\rho(\mathbf{r})\geq 0$, and electron number, $N$,
\begin{equation}
    e_{\text{x}}^{\text{FA}}(\mathbf{r})
    =
    -
    \frac{1}{2N}
    \int
    \frac
    {\rho(\mathbf{r})\rho(\mathbf{r}')}
    {|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|}
    d\mathbf{r}'.
\end{equation} 

For one-orbital systems (one-electron and two-electron singlet), two quantities are equal for all values of $\mathbf{r}$~\cite{Bosko.2023.JCP.159.131101,Bosko.2023.PRA.108.052803,Ayers.2005.MP.103.2061}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq: One-Orbital Relation}
    e_{\text{x}}^{\text{exact}}(\mathbf{r})
    =
    e_{\text{x}}^{\text{FA}}(\mathbf{r})
    \quad
    N\leq 2
    \quad
    \text{for all }
    \mathbf{r}.
\end{equation}
For systems with more than one molecular orbital, the spatial behavior of both quantities needs to be studied closer to establish a relation similar to Eq.~\eqref{eq: One-Orbital Relation}. Which is convenient to do analysing the corresponding exchange holes.
The exchange hole is defined via
\begin{equation}
    e_{\text{x}}(\mathbf{r})
    =
    \frac{1}{2}
    \int
    \frac
    {\rho(\mathbf{r})\rho_{\text{x}}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')}
    {|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|}
    d\mathbf{r}'
\end{equation}
leading to expressions for the exact and the Fermi--Amaldi exchange holes, respectively:
\begin{gather}
    \rho^{\text{exact}}_{\text{x}}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')
    =
    -
    \frac{1}{2}
    \frac
    {|\gamma(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')|^2}
    {\rho(\mathbf{r})}\label{eq: Exact Exchange Hole},\\[10pt]
    \rho^{\text{FA}}_{\text{x}}(\mathbf{r}')
    =
    -
    \frac{1}{N}
    \rho(\mathbf{r}')\label{eq: FA Exchange Hole}.
\end{gather}
% Both quantities are strictly non-positive, hence, we will consider their non-negative absolute values, $|\rho_{\text{x}}^{\text{exact}}(\mathbf{r})|$ and $|\rho_{\text{x}}^{\text{FA}}(\mathbf{r})|$, for convenience. 

When $N>2$, we cannot establish an unambiguous inequality between these exchnage holes for all $\mathbf{r}$. While, by Cauchy--Schwarz inequality:
\begin{equation}
    \rho(\mathbf{r})\rho(\mathbf{r}')
    \geq
    |\gamma(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')|^2
    \quad
    N>2
    \quad
    \text{for all }
    \mathbf{r},
\end{equation}
the Fermi--Amaldi prefactor $1/N$ decays faster than the constant prefactor $1/2$ of the exact exchange hole. Therefore, there are some renges of $|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|$ dominated by one hole and others dominated by another.

Next, in the limit of infinitesimaly small interelectronic distances ($r\to r'$) we have:
\begin{equation}
    |\gamma(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r})|^2
    =
    \rho(\mathbf{r})^2,
\end{equation}
consequently, the depths of the exchang holes are 
\begin{equation}
    \rho_{\text{x}}^{\text{exact}}(\mathbf{r})
    =
    -
    \frac{\rho(\mathbf{r})}{2}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
    \rho_{\text{x}}^{\text{FA}}(\mathbf{r})
    =
    -
    \frac{\rho(\mathbf{r})}{N}
\end{equation}
thus, for $N>2$, the well of the exchange hole is dominated by the exact exchange hole.

In the limit of infinitely large nterelectromic distances ($|r - r'| = \infty$)

Effectively, comparing equations~\eqref{eq: Exact Exchange Hole} and~\eqref{eq: FA Exchange Hole}, we are looking for zeros of the equation:
\begin{equation}
    N
    |\gamma(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')|^2
    -
    2
    \rho(\mathbf{r})
    \rho(\mathbf{r}')
    =
    0
    \quad\quad
    N>2,
\end{equation}

\section{SCRAP}

A local hybrid exchange functional is given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq: Local Hybrid Exchange}
    E^{\text{LH}}_{\text{x}}
    =
    \int
    \left\{
        g(\textbf{r})
        e_{\text{x}}^{\text{exact}}(\textbf{r})
        +
        [1-g(\textbf{r})]
        e_{\text{x}}^{\text{SL}}(\textbf{r})
    \right\}
    d\textbf{r},
\end{equation}
where the integrand is a mixture of two exchange density energies weighed by a local mixing function (LMF), $g(\mathbf{r})$, which is position-dependent. The $e_{\text{x}}^{\text{SL}}$ and $e_{\text{x}}^{\text{exact}}$ quantities are a semi-local and the exact exchange energy densities, respectively. 

A particular case of a one-electron limit, i.e., the asymptotic limit ($r\to\infty$) where the exact exchange must cancel out an excessive self-repulsion of the Coulomb potential, is also satisfied:
\begin{equation}
    \lim_{r\to\infty}
    \frac{e_{\text{x}}^{\text{FA}}}
    {e_{\text{x}}^{\text{exact}}}
    =
    1
\end{equation}
since both energy densities approach $-1/2r$ as $r\to\infty$.
